MILLIMETER LAB

More distance. Less effort.

  1. Drag A or B along its side of the beam. Distances snap every 25 mm.
  2. Select a weight to keep its green arrows visible. The left/right arrows move it; the up/down handle changes its mass when dragged. Click its + / − arrows for one 25 g step.
  3. Try 2× distance. Predict what happens when you halve the mass on the longer arm.
  4. Use Hide math for predictions, then reveal the live calculation.

Drag the table to orbit; scroll or pinch to zoom. A and B stay attached to their physical weights. Hold level keeps the beam still; release it to test balance.

Keyboard: Tab to a weight label. Left/right moves it across the screen; up/down changes mass. All values also have keyboard-accessible sliders and number boxes. Focus, tap, or hover on underlined math for an explanation; Escape closes a tooltip.

About the model: an ideal first-class lever, with the fulcrum between the effort and load. Only the two labeled masses count. The fixed, central axle has no friction; the beam and hangers have no mass in the calculation. This is a custom teaching apparatus, not a calibrated VEX build. Its ±12° travel is limited for visibility.

Distance: labels measure along the beam from the axle to each hanger. When tilted, both perpendicular moment arms shrink by the same factor, so the balance ratio is unchanged. The math panel compares turning effects at level.
